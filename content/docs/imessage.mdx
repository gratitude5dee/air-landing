Messages is Air's home. There is nothing to install and no new inbox to check — Air is a contact, and the thread is the whole product.

## What you can send

| You send | Air does |
| --- | --- |
| Plain text | Reads it in the context of the whole thread and replies, asks, or starts working. |
| Photos and screenshots | Looks at them. Reference images, whiteboards, receipts, UI screenshots all work. |
| Files (PDF, docs, spreadsheets) | Saves them to its [computer](/docs/computer) and reads them when relevant. |
| Links | Opens them in its own browser and reads the page. |
| Long, messy braindumps | Yes. Air is good at turning a wall of text into a plan. |

## Slash commands

A few shortcuts skip the small talk:

- `/imagine <prompt>` — generate an image and return it to the thread.
- `/animate <prompt>` — turn an image into a short animated clip.
- `/zap <what>` — a fast, one-shot task with no follow-up questions.

You never *need* a slash command; plain language does the same thing. They are there for when you already know exactly what you want.

## Typing, receipts, and timing

When Air is working you will see a read receipt and a typing indicator. Quick answers come back in seconds. Bigger jobs — reading a long deck, building something on its computer, generating video — take longer, and Air will usually say so up front. You can keep texting; it queues your messages and picks them up in order.

## Cards and mini-apps

Some replies are richer than text. Air sends **cards** that open into small, focused screens inside Messages — a kanban board, a to-do list, a calendar week, a draft email, a product to buy. These are [mini-apps](/docs/mini-apps). Tap to open, act, and come back to the thread; nothing leaves Messages.

## Approvals in the thread

When Air needs your sign-off it tells you precisely what it wants to do:

> *Ready to send to sam@studio.example — subject "Shoot dates, confirmed." Reply **yes** to send, or tell me what to change.*

Reply in plain language. **yes** / **send it** / **go** approves. **no** / **hold** declines. Anything else is treated as feedback and Air revises. The same approvals also appear in the **Needs you** queue in the web app if you would rather batch them.

## Other people texting Air

Air's number is yours. If someone else texts it, Air does **not** reply. Instead you get a note in the thread and in **Needs you** telling you who wrote and what they said, and you decide whether to let that person through. Once you do, Air can handle scheduling and back-and-forth with them on your behalf, always inside the rules you set. See [FAQ: trust tiers](/docs/faq#who-can-talk-to-my-air).

## The web twin

The thread is mirrored live at [app.wzrd.tech](https://app.wzrd.tech). It is the same conversation with the same memory — start something on your phone, finish it on your laptop, or the other way round.
