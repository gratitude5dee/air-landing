Air is a personal creative assistant that lives in your iMessages. It has its own phone number, its own email inbox, and its own computer — and it works for exactly one person: you.

You text it the way you would text a collaborator. It reads what you send, does the work on its own machine, and brings the result back to the thread. When something would leave a mark on the outside world — sending an email, spending money, posting somewhere — it asks you first.

## The three things Air has

- **A phone number.** Air is a contact in Messages. There is no app to install; the thread is the interface. See [iMessage](/docs/imessage).
- **An inbox.** Air gets an email address you can share. Mail to that address becomes work in your thread, and Air drafts replies for you to approve. See [Email](/docs/email).
- **A computer.** Every Air runs on its own private machine with a browser, a file system, and the tools it installs for you. Nothing is shared between users. See [Computer](/docs/computer).

## How a request moves

1. You text Air an outcome, a reference, or a rough brief.
2. Air works on its computer — reading, browsing, drafting, generating.
3. Anything that only needs you to *see* it comes straight back to the thread.
4. Anything that would *act* on your behalf waits in **Needs you** until you approve it.

That fourth step is the whole design. Air can be as capable as a real assistant because it never gets to skip the part where you say yes.

## What Air is not

- **Not a group chat bot.** Air answers you. Messages from people it does not know are held for your review instead of getting a reply.
- **Not a dashboard you have to check.** The web app at `app.wzrd.tech` exists and mirrors your thread, but it is optional. Everything important reaches you in Messages.
- **Not a shared model with shared memory.** Your history, files, and connections live in your own Air and nowhere else.
